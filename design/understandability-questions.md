# Understandability pass

Controlling feedback: Maz's four-page September 6 PDF and Sarah's direction: no deep menus or complex navigation choices; keep the focus on guided intake and make it easy for the user. These questions were written after capturing the current pages at 1440px and before opening their source for this redesign.

## Requester intake

1. What should I tell One Door about my work?
2. What is happening after I submit that description, and can I safely leave?
3. What does One Door still need to know from me?
4. What did my answers change, and what is my next step?

Keep the evolving request on one page. Do not ask the requester to choose a service, architecture, or internal workflow. Preserve the single optional whole-need strong-fit offer and the explicit final send to OIT.

## Reviewer queue

1. Which requests can I work on now?
2. What needs doing on each request, and which should I open first?
3. How can I find or sort a request without losing where I was?

The first demo view needs believable assigned examples, including scored and unscored work. A display must not misstate who owns a request or overwrite another visitor's assignments.

## Reviewing a request

1. What decision am I making in this section?
2. What facts about the request and proposed solution support that decision?
3. What have I already recorded, and what still needs attention?
4. How do I record my decision and continue?

One section at a time, with a clear next step. Historic feedback must not precede the current decision. A match needs both the product's actual purpose and its specific coverage of this request.

## RICE

1. Who benefits, and how many are we counting?
2. How much better will their work become?
3. How certain are those estimates?
4. How much work will delivery take, and who checked that estimate?
5. What score will be saved, and can I leave an unfinished estimate safely?

Use everyday questions and concrete examples. People enter the estimates; the app calculates. Effort must use an understandable input while preserving a consistent calculation and existing saved scores.

## Completing review

1. Which review decisions are still unfinished?
2. Who takes responsibility for delivery, and what must they do?
3. What does completing review change, and what does it not mean?

Distinguish people reviewing the request from the person or team delivering the result. Do not combine a person's identity and a task in one unstructured field.

## Shared controls

Show design-note controls only when the current page has notes. Put sorting on table headings, keep the current order visible, and sort all matching records before pagination. Retain save, error, keyboard, and recovery behavior throughout.
